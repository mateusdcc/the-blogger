There is a specific cost to being perceived. Not only the cost of socializing,
but the cost of submitting a version of the self for feedback and then trying to
act as if the feedback did not matter.

The need for attention is often described as vanity, which is a convenient way
to stop thinking. Vanity is only one of attention's costumes. More often,
attention is a request for confirmation that a version of the self is working.

## The transaction

The exchange usually looks like this:

1. I offer an image of myself.
2. You return a signal that the image arrived.
3. I temporarily feel continuous.
4. The feeling decays.
5. I offer another image.

The loop is not necessarily pathological. People need feedback. The problem
begins when feedback becomes the only evidence that a self is coherent.

> The attention economy did not invent insecurity. It made insecurity easier to
> measure.

What makes attention strange is that it never becomes property. It has to be
renewed, and every renewal teaches the self something about what is rewarded.

## A small result

When attention is scarce, people become strategic. When attention is abundant,
people become dependent. The difficult question is not whether attention is good
or bad, but what kind of self it trains into existence.
