Narcissism is usually described as loving oneself too much. I think that
description misses something. The narcissistic structure often looks less like
an excess of self-love than a dependence on external confirmation. The self
cannot easily stabilize itself without importing evidence from the outside.

Admiration arrives, but it does not settle the problem for very long.

## The maintenance cost

The narcissistic exchange is recursive:

- I perform superiority to obtain admiration.
- The admiration confirms the performance, not the self.
- Because the self remains untouched, more performance is required.
- The cost increases because the performance must keep surpassing its previous
  version.

This is why praise can become expensive. Praise attaches to the image, and the
image then has to be maintained.

## The ordinary version

Most people have some smaller version of this mechanism. We want to be loved for
what we are, while quietly editing what can be seen. We want recognition without
exposure. We want intimacy with favorable lighting.

There is no clean exit from this. The useful move is noticing when the image
starts negotiating on behalf of the person.
