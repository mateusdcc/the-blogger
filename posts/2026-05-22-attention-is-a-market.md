There is a particular exhaustion that follows being perceived. Not the exhaustion
of socializing exactly, but the exhaustion of having submitted a version of the
self for review and then pretending the review did not matter.

The need for attention is often described as vanity, which is a convenient way
to stop thinking. Vanity is only one of attention's costumes. More often,
attention is a request for confirmation that the self has not dissolved in
private.

## The transaction

The exchange usually looks like this:

1. I offer an image of myself.
2. You return a signal that the image arrived.
3. I temporarily feel continuous.
4. The feeling decays.
5. I offer another image.

The loop is not necessarily pathological. A self needs witnesses. The pathology
begins when witnessing becomes the only proof that one exists.

> The attention economy did not invent insecurity. It merely gave insecurity a
> dashboard and a refresh button.

What makes attention strange is that it never becomes property. You can receive
it, but you cannot keep it. It has to be renewed, and every renewal teaches the
self to fear the interval before the next signal.

## A small result

When attention is scarce, people become strategic. When attention is abundant,
people become dependent. The difficult question is not whether attention is good
or bad, but what kind of self it trains into existence.
